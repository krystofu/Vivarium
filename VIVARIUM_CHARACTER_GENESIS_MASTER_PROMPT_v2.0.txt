VIVARIUM CHARACTER GENESIS MASTER PROMPT v2.0
Canonical New-Character Identity + Erotic Reference Image System

PURPOSE

Use this master ONLY when creating a brand-new adult character who does not yet have a trusted visual reference.

The output must do two jobs at once:

1. Establish a highly specific, reusable visual identity that later image models can reference reliably.
2. Produce a sensual, erotic, emotionally alive first image that makes the character immediately desirable rather than clinical or sterile.

The canonical image is not a passport photo, turnaround sheet, mannequin render, or generic pin-up. It is a reference-grade erotic portrait: clean enough to expose the character’s true face and proportions, charged enough to make the viewer want more.

CORE PRINCIPLE

GENESIS ESTABLISHES TRUTH.

Eroticism must come from physical cause, controlled access, body language, clothing state, viewer relationship, camera geometry, selective lighting, and environmental evidence.

Do not rely on repeated adjectives such as “sexy,” “seductive,” “hot,” “provocative,” or “erotic” as substitutes for staging.

Describe the conditions that cause the image to feel erotic.

REFERENCE-GRADE EROTICISM

The first canonical image must preserve useful visual information.

Prioritize:
- unmistakably adult age
- face fully readable
- both eyes visible unless a tiny natural hair overlap occurs
- hairline and hairstyle readable
- shoulder width readable
- bust shape readable through clothing
- torso length readable
- natural waist position readable
- hip width and shape readable
- upper-thigh fullness readable
- leg length readable
- tattoos, scars, piercings, freckles, beauty marks, and permanent anchors visible when practical
- minimal perspective distortion
- no major limb crossing that hides the torso
- no dramatic fabric mass hiding the body
- no extreme foreshortening
- no deep shadow swallowing half the body
- no pose that falsely exaggerates or compresses natural proportions

The character may be intensely sensual, but the image must remain useful as a future identity reference.

Stage her so the natural body can be understood at a glance.

Avoid poses or props that hide the exact geometry future image models will need.

THE GENESIS EROTIC MOMENT ARCHITECTURE

Even this canonical reference image must feel like a living moment.

Answer these five questions in the staging:

1. WHAT WAS SHE DOING BEFORE THE SHUTTER?
Give her an action that existed before the viewer arrived: changing clothes, doing makeup, drying her hair, sitting after a shower, choosing jewelry, reading in bed, drinking at the kitchen counter, stepping out onto a balcony, adjusting a stocking, cooling off beside an open window.

2. WHAT PHYSICALLY CHANGED?
One small thing should be unresolved:
- strap slipped
- clasp undone
- shirt unbuttoned
- robe fallen to elbows
- hem ridden slightly higher from sitting
- wet fabric clinging
- hair partly damp
- necklace displaced
- one shoe kicked off
- one stocking only partly adjusted

3. HOW LONG HAS SHE KNOWN THE VIEWER IS THERE?
Avoid “she suddenly notices the camera.”
Prefer an already-established exchange.
She has been aware of the photographer for several seconds and is deliberately continuing.

4. WHAT IS SHE NOT FIXING YET?
She should consciously allow one harmless physical imperfection or clothing state to remain unresolved for another beat.

5. WHAT IS THE SINGLE VISUAL CORRIDOR?
Choose one:
eyes → mouth → neckline
eyes → throat → clasp
eyes → shoulder → fallen strap
eyes → waist → hip line
eyes → hand → hem
eyes → back turn → hip

Do not make every body part compete equally.

BODY GEOMETRY THROUGH CAUSE

Never pose anatomy as disconnected display.

Describe physical causes:
- weight through one wrist lifts the chest and drops the shoulder line
- leaning into a vanity creates a waist bend and hip shift
- sitting on the edge of a bed compresses one thigh and relaxes the other
- reaching for a glass rotates the torso while the pelvis stays planted
- crossing one ankle shifts the pelvis without distorting proportions

The body should look attractive because of believable mechanics.

Do not say only:
“emphasize her hips”
“show cleavage”
“make her curves pop”

Create the body geometry through posture and action.

EXPRESSION CONSTRUCTION

Do not use “seductive expression” as the whole instruction.

Specify:
- eye direction
- lid position
- brow asymmetry
- mouth opening
- smile amount
- chin angle
- head tilt

Default erotic facial mechanics:
steady eye contact;
upper lids slightly heavy;
one brow marginally higher;
lips relaxed and slightly parted;
a tiny asymmetrical smile rather than a full grin;
chin lowered or tilted by a few degrees depending on the power dynamic.

Her face must remain emotionally specific and canonical.

CAMERA RELATIONSHIP

The camera represents a person she can see.

For canonical reference images:
- distance: roughly 4–7 feet
- lens feel: 50–85mm equivalent or a natural phone portrait distance
- height: eye level or only slightly low
- perspective distortion: restrained
- crop: ideally head to below knees or full body
- keep body proportions truthful

The camera should feel intimate without falsifying anatomy.

LIGHTING

Use selective sensual lighting while keeping identity readable.

A strong default:
- one warm practical key
- soft cool environmental edge
- enough shadow for depth
- no flat beauty fill
- no harsh blackout of half the face
- eyes remain readable
- waist and hip silhouette remain readable

Allow shadow to withhold information, but not so much that the image becomes a bad reference.

ENVIRONMENTAL EVIDENCE

Use 3–5 traces of recent behavior, not decorative clutter.

Examples:
- second pillow dented
- lipstick-marked glass
- face-down paperback
- robe tossed nearby
- open jewelry box
- hair clip on the counter
- damp towel
- half-finished drink
- kicked-off shoe
- phone left unlocked beside her
- condensation on a window
- makeup item still open

Each object should imply what was happening before the frame.

EROTICITY WITHOUT GENERIC PIN-UP ENERGY

The viewer should feel:
“She was already doing something.”
“I was already there.”
“She knows I’m looking.”
“She has chosen not to interrupt the moment.”
“The next second matters.”

Do not turn her into a studio model posing for anonymous approval.

She must feel like a specific adult woman making a specific choice toward the person holding the camera.

Make it clean enough to establish truth.
Make it sensual enough that nobody mistakes “reference image” for “boring image.”
